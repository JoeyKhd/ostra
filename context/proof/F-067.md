Notes (2026-11-20, milestone 5's ticket 13): the admission is `queue.admit` keyed by pool (`admitQueued` in `packages/domain/src/use-cases/admission.ts`), plus the sweep every `OSTRA_QUEUE_SWEEP_SECONDS`. Each task is its own transaction (`dispatchTask`): it takes the task's row, then the project's pools' rows in ID order, counts the boxes from `Box` rows, chooses (`chooseRunner`) and grants, or records the reason with the task's place. The race test fires all fifty admissions at once from three Prisma clients (each a replica's connection pool), every task tried by each, then each replica's pass of the queue, and counts the boxes from rows afterwards. The second row stays `partial` until an admin can see the queue in the panel (`fleet.queue`, ticket 26; the page, ticket 33): the queue's order and the cancel are proved here.

Notes (2026-11-20, milestone 5's ticket 26): admins read a pool's queue through `pools.queue` (each task with its project, priority, queued time, wait reason and place) and cancel from it through `tasks.cancelQueued`, which a task's member may call for their own; `tasks.get` and `tasks.list` give each queued task its place now (`queue`), read from the queue itself rather than the place the admission last recorded. The second row stays `partial` until the pool's page shows the queue (tickets 30 and 33).

Notes (2026-11-20, milestone 5's ticket 33): a queued task's page says its place in its pool's queue (`3rd in build-fleet's queue`, from `tasks.get`'s `queue`) and why it waits, one sentence per reason from `messages/en.json`, and a task cancelled from the queue says who cancelled it.
