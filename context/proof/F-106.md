## Notes

- **Ticket 06 (2026-11-20)** built the pure parts: ostra's own five-field evaluator on `Intl` (`packages/domain/src/automations/cron.ts`), checked over every minute of 2026 in `UTC`, `Europe/Amsterdam` and `America/New_York` against a reference the test computes minute by minute, and against the daylight-saving days written out by hand; the missed rule (`planOccurrences`); the minimum interval; the automation's and the occurrence's lifecycles; the jobs `scheduler.tick` and `automation.occurrence` with their keys. Ticket 05's migration holds the unique occurrence key. The rows stay `partial` until ticket 10's tick inserts each occurrence with its job in one transaction, reads the `Clock` port and audits the missed ones, and ticket 37's hunt kills the replicas.
- The minimum interval is a working value of the save (ticket 24); its test asserts each side of the hour.
- **Ticket 10 (2026-11-20)** built the tick and the occurrences (`packages/domain/src/automations/scheduler.ts`, core's workers in `apps/core/src/automations/automations.ts`), tested against Postgres and pg-boss with the `Clock` port moved by hand. Each due schedule is read `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` and its occurrence's row, its `automation.occurrence` job and the advanced `nextOccurrenceAt` commit together. Two ticks on three database clients never offer the same occurrence (the lock and the re-checked due time), so the `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` is the backstop for a tick that finds its occurrence recorded (a schedule's row set back, as a restore would): the parallel test sets it back once, which is what the break-it fails.
- The gates (milestone 4's budget, milestone 5's labels and a project's share of a pool) are checked by the real dispatch on the occurrence's task: it stays queued with each gate's reason (`apps/core/src/testing/start-gates.ts`). An automation's pool labels reach admission at every queueing (`starts/origin-labels.ts`, used by the dispatch and `queueTask`).
- "A tick's resolution is one minute" is the pg-boss schedule's `* * * * *`, registered once whichever replicas start, and a tick seconds after a due time fires it; how late a start comes on a live install is the hunt's (ticket 37), so the row stays `partial`. The hunt's row (killing every replica but one) is ticket 37's and stays `missing`.
- Ticket 10's other break-its, each restored: `runOccurrence` skipping its re-read of the automation (the pause test failed), and the prompt schedule's start returning `filter` (the workers' test found no task).
- **Ticket 24 (2026-11-20)** gave schedules their API: `automations.previewSchedule` (the sentence from the domain's `cronSentence`, the next three occurrences in the zone, or the refusal under `OSTRA_SCHEDULE_MIN_INTERVAL_MINUTES`), and `create`, `update`, `pause`, `resume` and `delete` for a project admin through ticket 10's use cases. A delete is soft: the automation leaves the list and its `history` stays readable to the project's members.
