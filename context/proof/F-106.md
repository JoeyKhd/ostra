## Notes

- **Ticket 06 (2026-11-20)** built the pure parts: ostra's own five-field evaluator on `Intl` (`packages/domain/src/automations/cron.ts`), checked over every minute of 2026 in `UTC`, `Europe/Amsterdam` and `America/New_York` against a reference the test computes minute by minute, and against the daylight-saving days written out by hand; the missed rule (`planOccurrences`); the minimum interval; the automation's and the occurrence's lifecycles; the jobs `scheduler.tick` and `automation.occurrence` with their keys. Ticket 05's migration holds the unique occurrence key. The rows stay `partial` until ticket 10's tick inserts each occurrence with its job in one transaction, reads the `Clock` port and audits the missed ones, and ticket 37's hunt kills the replicas.
- The minimum interval is a working value of the save (ticket 24); its test asserts each side of the hour.
- **Ticket 10 (2026-11-20)** built the tick and the occurrences (`packages/domain/src/automations/scheduler.ts`, core's workers in `apps/core/src/automations/automations.ts`), tested against Postgres and pg-boss with the `Clock` port moved by hand. Each due schedule is read `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` and its occurrence's row, its `automation.occurrence` job and the advanced `nextOccurrenceAt` commit together. Two ticks on three database clients never offer the same occurrence (the lock and the re-checked due time), so the `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` is the backstop for a tick that finds its occurrence recorded (a schedule's row set back, as a restore would): the parallel test sets it back once, which is what the break-it fails.
- The gates (milestone 4's budget, milestone 5's labels and a project's share of a pool) are checked by the real dispatch on the occurrence's task: it stays queued with each gate's reason (`apps/core/src/testing/start-gates.ts`). An automation's pool labels reach admission at every queueing (`starts/origin-labels.ts`, used by the dispatch and `queueTask`).
- "A tick's resolution is one minute" is the pg-boss schedule's `* * * * *`, registered once whichever replicas start, and a tick seconds after a due time fires it; how late a start comes on a live install is the hunt's (ticket 37), so the row stays `partial`.
- Ticket 10's other break-its, each restored: `runOccurrence` skipping its re-read of the automation (the pause test failed), and the prompt schedule's start returning `filter` (the workers' test found no task).
- **Ticket 24 (2026-11-20)** gave schedules their API: `automations.previewSchedule` (the sentence from the domain's `cronSentence`, the next three occurrences in the zone, or the refusal under `OSTRA_SCHEDULE_MIN_INTERVAL_MINUTES`), and `create`, `update`, `pause`, `resume` and `delete` for a project admin through ticket 10's use cases. A delete is soft: the automation leaves the list and its `history` stays readable to the project's members.
- **Ticket 37 (2026-11-20), the hunt:** spike 8's C4 hunt against the tick (`apps/core/src/schedule-hunt.test.ts` and `src/testing/schedule-hunt.ts`, run by `pnpm e2e:schedule-hunt` and the failure suite's `schedule-hunt` case). 200 automations, `* * * * *`, saved at runtime on one database with three core processes (`restart-child.ts`, the real `startCore`). For each tick every replica but one is armed at one kill point (`apps/core/src/kill-points.ts`): `scheduler.tick-written` inside a schedule's tick transaction (its occurrence, job and next time written, before the commit), `automation.occurrence-started` inside the occurrence's transaction (its task started, before the commit), or `automation.occurrence-committed` (after that commit, before the job completes). The armed replica that reaches the point first, the one holding the work, ends itself there with SIGKILL from inside the transaction (a test hook, the domain's `onStep`, never the clock), and the harness kills the other armed one on the line it prints as it dies; the survivor rotates. Then a late start: every process stopped, and started again with the scheduler's clock 7 hours ahead.
- **Three runs in a row passed** on 2026-11-20 (Windows 11, Node 24, embedded Postgres), 17 to 19 minutes each: 6 strikes landed in 6 or 7 ticks (2 at each point), and 1,600, 1,600 and 1,200 (automation, occurrence) pairs each started exactly once, with one `automation_run` row, one task (queued, no runner) and one `automation.started` event, and no minute without its row; after 7 hours down, all 200 automations fired once for the latest occurrence, the rest recorded `missed`, with one audit entry each holding the count. The third run's log is `context/specs/m6-whole-team/demo/schedule-hunt.log`.
- **The control failed each time, as spike 8 found** (`packages/jobs/src/testing/cron-control.ts`): the same 200 schedules on pg-boss's own cron with its defaults, three replicas, hunted as spike 8 hunted it (the forwarding queue polled back to back, every replica but one killed when a batch is active). Its stranded batches came back after pg-boss's 15-minute expiry and the replay started occurrences a second time: 68, 68 and 106 duplicate starts, and in the third run 139 of 600 pairs missed their tick. A first attempt's control killed through `taskkill` only, which takes longer than a batch lasts: it stranded nothing in 5 strikes and passed, so that attempt failed as a whole, and the replicas are now killed at once (`child.kill`) before the tree.
- **Break-it:** `runOccurrence`'s check that a replayed job finds its occurrence decided was cut to `run.state !== "started"` (the `taskId !== null` part dropped). The hunt's `automation.occurrence-committed` strike replayed the job, 3 of 90 pairs got two tasks, and the hunt failed its "exactly once" and "one task per occurrence" checks (a 30-automation run). Restored before the three runs.
- **What the hunt measured of "a start comes a few seconds late":** the occurrence's row is written 7.9, 21.2 and 8.2 s after its minute at p50 in the three runs, and 23.3, 34.1 and 21.8 s at p95. pg-boss's cron pass, which sends the tick's own job, runs every 30 s (`cronMonitorIntervalSeconds`), so a tick can come up to about half a minute late, and more after a strike at the tick (its job comes back on a missed heartbeat). That row stays `partial`: a few seconds holds at the median only.
- `minimumIntervalOk` walked a year of minutes for every save; at one minute (the hunt's schedules) it now answers at once, and its test has the case.
